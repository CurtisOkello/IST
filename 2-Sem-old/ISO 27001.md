### The Mock Gap Assessment Methodology for Today

A **Gap Assessment** (recall Day 9's Step 1) is an internal, pre-certification exercise comparing an organization's current state against the full ISO 27001 requirement set — the exact exercise you will conduct on Bidii SACCO today. A properly structured Gap Assessment Report contains:

1. **Executive Summary** — a short, plain-language overview of overall readiness and the most significant gaps, written for a non-technical audience (recall Day 4's report-writing standard).
2. **Scope Statement** — precisely what was and wasn't assessed (recall Day 4's planning content).
3. **Findings by Theme** — organized across Organizational, People, Physical, and Technological controls (Days 7–8), each finding stated factually, evidenced, and graded (Day 4).
4. **Risk Summary** — the highest-priority risks, each with a stated treatment decision and named owner (Day 11).
5. **Statement of Applicability Extract** — at least a representative sample of control applicability decisions with justification (Day 11).
6. **Recommended Roadmap** — a realistic, sequenced set of next steps toward Stage 1 readiness (Day 9).

## Multi-Perspective Breakdown — The Module in Full

**Attacker's POV, across the whole module:** Every case study this module examined — from Equifax's unpatched Struts server to Uber's hardcoded AWS credentials — shares one uncomfortable throughline: the attacker rarely needed a novel technique. In nearly every case, the organization's own documentation, if it had existed and been honestly acted upon, already described the exact weakness that was exploited. An attacker's real advantage is almost never technical sophistication alone — it's that organizations consistently know less about their own environment, or act less on what they do know, than they assume.

**Defender's / SOC Analyst's POV, across the whole module:** Every technical skill you built in Module 8 — Sysmon hunting, SIEM correlation, network segmentation — has a specific, numbered home in this module's Annex A content (Days 7–8). A SOC Analyst who understands this module doesn't just detect incidents; they can trace any given detection capability back to the specific governance requirement it satisfies, and can speak fluently to auditors, management, and GRC colleagues about why their technical work matters at the level of formal risk treatment (Day 11), not just at the level of a blocked IP address.

**Auditor's / Forensic Investigator's POV, across the whole module:** This entire module has been building toward one professional capability: the ability to look at any organization and systematically answer four questions — What information matters here (Day 1–2)? What could threaten it, and how would we know (Day 3–5)? What controls should exist, and do they genuinely work (Days 6–8)? And if a gap exists, what's the accountable, documented plan to close it (Days 9–11)? Today's mock gap assessment is your first full rehearsal of asking and answering all four questions on a single organization, start to finish.

## Case Study Marathon — Every Module 9 Case, One Pattern Each

Run through this table rapidly as a class before beginning the lab — for each case, the goal is not to re-read the full story, but to instantly recall the **one core pattern** it illustrates.

| Case | Day Introduced | Core Pattern It Illustrates |
|---|---|---|
| **Equifax (2017)** | Day 1 | A patchable technical gap becomes catastrophic specifically because of what *information* sat behind it — the Information layer, not the System layer, determines real severity. |
| **Target / Fazio Mechanical (2013)** | Day 2 | The Partners "P" is consistently under-scrutinized — a compromise can enter through the weakest connected third party, not the strongest internal control. |
| **Colonial Pipeline (2021)** | Day 3 | A catastrophic incident can trace back to one missing, routine, entirely checkable security parameter (MFA on one legacy account) — not a sophisticated exploit. |
| **SolarWinds (2020)** | Day 4 | Audit scope itself is a risk decision — excluding "trusted vendor updates" from scrutiny left an entire ecosystem blind to a supply-chain compromise. |
| **Wells Fargo Fake Accounts** | Day 4 | Document review alone cannot catch a policy that's routinely ignored under organizational pressure — only observation, re-performance, and CAATs can. |
| **Arthur Andersen / Enron (2001)** | Day 5 | Independence in appearance is a necessary safeguard on its own — a conflicted structure undermines trust even before, and regardless of whether, it corrupts an individual's actual judgment. |
| **TalkTalk (2015)** | Day 6 | A Tier 1 policy with no genuine Tier 2/Tier 3 evidence underneath it offers close to zero actual protection. |
| **Twitter Celebrity Hack (2020)** | Day 7 | Organizational (segregation of duties, access rights) and People (training, remote work) control gaps combine to turn one social-engineered credential into a massive-scale incident. |
| **Capital One (2019)** | Day 8 | Least Privilege applies to service/application roles, not just human accounts — a misconfigured permission can be exploited with no phishing at all. |
| **U.S. Veterans Affairs Laptop Theft (2006)** | Day 8 | A purely Physical Controls gap (an unencrypted device leaving the premises) can undo otherwise-reasonable Technological controls entirely. |
| **Marriott / Starwood (2018)** | Day 9 | Periodic, sample-based assurance (certification surveillance, PCI-style cycles) structurally cannot guarantee catching a long-dwelling compromise between checkpoints. |
| **British Airways IT Outage (2017)** | Day 10 | High E-Business Intensity demands *tested*, not merely documented, resilience — an untested DR plan is not a real control. |
| **GitLab Database Incident (2017)** | Day 10 | Documentation of a control (five backup mechanisms) means nothing without a genuine evaluation methodology (re-performance) proving it actually works. |
| **Uber Breach & Cover-Up (2016)** | Day 11 | Concealment is never a legitimate fifth risk treatment option — it converts a containable technical finding into a governance and legal catastrophe. |

**Marathon synthesis question for class discussion before the lab begins:** Looking at all fourteen cases together, which single failure type appears most often — a technical vulnerability, a documentation gap, a governance/accountability failure, or an evaluation/testing gap? Notice how few of these fourteen cases were actually caused by a truly novel, unstoppable technical attack.

## Interactive Lab — The Full Mock ISO 27001 Gap Assessment (Extended Session)

**Lab Title:** Bidii SACCO — Final Mock Gap Assessment Report

**Objective:** Combine every artifact built across Days 1–11 into a single, complete, professionally-structured Gap Assessment Report for Bidii SACCO — the capstone deliverable for Module 9.

**Required Environment / Tools:** Every Bidii SACCO artifact produced this module: the Day 1 Asset Register, Day 2 classification/4 Ps table, Day 3 Threat-Vulnerability-Risk-Parameter chains, Day 4 audit plan, Day 5 independence scenario notes, Day 6 three-tier documentation stack, Days 7–8 Annex A mapping tables, Day 9 certification roadmap, Day 10 E-Business Intensity rating and ALE calculation, and Day 11's Risk Register and SoA extract.

**Note on class format:** For this capstone, both pairs merge into a single four-person "assessment team," mirroring a real small audit engagement team. Assign roles explicitly using Day 5's vocabulary: one student as **Lead Auditor** (overall report ownership and final sign-off), two as **Audit Team Members** (one focused on Organizational/People findings, one on Physical/Technological findings), and one as **Technical Expert** (supporting both, and specifically responsible for sanity-checking the Day 10 quantitative risk figures and Day 11 SoA entries for consistency).

**Step-by-Step Execution Guide:**

1. **(5 min)** As a team, agree and write a formal **Scope Statement** for this mock assessment, drawing on every system and process named in the Bidii SACCO scenario across the module (member portal, core banking application, physical filing room, HR system, WhatsApp group, decommissioned pilot server, SMS gateway vendor).

2. **(10 min)** The two Organizational/People and Physical/Technological Team Members each pull their strongest three findings from their respective Days 7–8 mapping tables, and restate each as a fully formed, gradable finding (Finding Statement, Evidence, Grade) using Day 4's format.

3. **(10 min)** As a team, take the three highest-severity findings overall and convert each into a full Day 11-style Risk Register entry — Asset, Threat, Vulnerability, CIA property, Likelihood, Impact, Risk Rating, named Risk Owner, Treatment decision, and related control(s).

4. **(5 min)** The Technical Expert leads the team through inserting the Day 10 E-Business Intensity rating and at least one ALE calculation into the report as supporting justification for the highest-priority risk's treatment decision.

5. **(5 min)** As a team, draft a 4–6 row **Statement of Applicability extract** covering the controls most directly relevant to your top three risks, including at least one "Not Applicable" entry with genuine justification.

6. **(10 min)** The Lead Auditor compiles all of the above into a single **Executive Summary** (3–5 sentences, non-technical) and a **Recommended Roadmap** (3–4 sequenced next steps, referencing Day 9's certification lifecycle — e.g., "resolve the two Major findings before scheduling Stage 1").

7. **(5 min) Class presentation.** The Lead Auditor presents the complete report to the instructor, acting as the Audit Client. The instructor will ask at least one challenge question per major section (scope, findings, risk treatment, SoA) exactly as a real client or certification decision panel would.

**Expected Final Artifact:**

```
BIDII SACCO — MOCK GAP ASSESSMENT REPORT

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: ...

2. SCOPE: ...

3. FINDINGS BY THEME:
   Organizational: [Finding, Evidence, Grade] x3
   People: [Finding, Evidence, Grade] x as relevant
   Physical: [Finding, Evidence, Grade] x as relevant
   Technological: [Finding, Evidence, Grade] x as relevant

4. RISK SUMMARY (Top 3, full Risk Register format): ...

5. STATEMENT OF APPLICABILITY EXTRACT: ...

6. RECOMMENDED ROADMAP:
   Step 1: ...
   Step 2: ...
   Step 3: ...
```
